If you've bought leads before, you know how it goes.

The phone rings while you're up a ladder. A form comes in at 8 pm and nobody sees it until morning. Someone books an estimate, then doesn't answer the door. Your estimator drives across town to price one bedroom.

None of that is a lead problem. It's what happens between the lead and the estimate. Buy more leads into the same gaps and you lose more of them, at a higher price.

So this letter isn't about getting you more leads. It covers the four places a painting pipeline usually leaks, what each one costs you, and what we do instead. At the end there's a link to book a 30-minute review of your own pipeline.

the 4 leaks

Where the pipeline loses homeownersin the order a lead hits them

A homeowner passes four points on the way to an estimate: they see an ad, they reach out, they get booked, and they show up. Here's what usually goes wrong at each one.

Service-Area Demand Capture

Leak 1 of 4 · the ad
Most painters
Run broad "painters near me" ads or buy shared leads, and forget the customers they already have.
Why it hurts
Broad ads pull in small touch-ups, far-off addresses and price shoppers. Shared leads get called by three other painters. Old estimates and past customers sit in a spreadsheet nobody opens.
What it costs you
Ad money spent on homeowners you'd never quote, and repeat work going to whoever reached them last.
What we do instead
Meta ads built around the project types that make you money, in the neighbourhoods you want to work. And we reactivate your own list of past leads and customers, people who already know your name.
So you can
Fill the calendar with the work you want, close to home.

Instant Inquiry Response

Leak 2 of 4 · the first reply
Most painters
Call back when they get off the ladder, or the next morning.
Why it hurts
A homeowner who fills out a form is often asking more than one painter. The one who answers first usually gets the conversation.
What it costs you
You paid for the lead, then handed it to the painter who replied faster.
What we do instead
New calls and form inquiries get a fast reply, including evenings and weekends. Miss a call and the homeowner gets a text back.
So you can
Stay on site without letting the lead go cold.

Job Qualification & Routing

Leak 3 of 4 · the booking
Most painters
Book every estimate that asks, then find out the details in the driveway.
Why it hurts
Without scope, address and timing up front, your estimator drives to projects that are too small, too far away or a year out.
What it costs you
An afternoon of estimating time on a quote that was never going to sign.
What we do instead
We collect scope, address, timing and fit before the appointment is set. It lands on your calendar only when it matches the work you do.
So you can
Send your estimator to homes worth the drive.

Confirmation & Recovery

Leak 4 of 4 · the visit
Most painters
Book the estimate and hope the homeowner is home.
Why it hurts
People forget, reschedule or go quiet. Nobody chases them, because everyone is busy painting.
What it costs you
Empty driveways, and homeowners who were interested but never got booked.
What we do instead
We confirm and remind every appointment, follow up no-shows to rebook them, and keep following up with homeowners who didn't book.
So you can
Knock on doors that open.

Done-for-You guarantee

Customer copy

What we commit to

15 qualified attended estimate appointments in the first 30 days. Appointments generated through database reactivation count toward the same 15 if they meet the same qualification, attendance, and CRM-recording criteria. If EstimateFlows misses the target, it will continue work at no additional service fee for another 30 days; if the same 15-appointment target is still unmet at the end of that extension, refund all program fees and reimburse all client ad spend. This is not a guarantee of signed or completed painting jobs.

Conditions

Done-for-You only. Available only if you provide your complete historical lead list before campaign launch. Your side: at least $2,000 per month of ad spend paid to the advertising platform; the complete historical lead list before launch; at least 10 calls per week to eligible prior painting customers; reasonably open estimate capacity; keep your service areas and advertised work available; run and reschedule estimates in good faith and follow up on quotes; keep the approved response and booking operations active; give reasonable access, approvals, assets and timely creative review; capture before, mid-job and after photos or video for each job. Full terms are in your written agreement.

What counts

A qualified attended estimate appointment means: the project matches the agreed service area, project type and homeowner intent; the homeowner (or decision-maker) and your estimator attend and the estimate is conducted. Bookings, cancellations and no-shows don't count.
